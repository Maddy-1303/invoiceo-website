How it works

From setup to payment in five steps

Invoiceo follows the way a shop already bills — just faster and tidier.

  1. 01

    Set up your business

    Add your company name, logo, GSTIN, bank details and UPI ID once.

  2. 02

    Add customers and products

    Import a list from CSV, or add them as you bill. Barcode scanners work too.

  3. 03

    Create an invoice

    Pick the customer and search or scan items. Tax and totals are worked out for you.

  4. 04

    Print, save or share

    Print on A4 or a thermal printer, or save a PDF to send to your customer.

  5. 05

    Track payments

    Record full or part payments, print receipts and see who still owes you.

FAQ

Questions people ask before downloading

Is Invoiceo really free?

Yes. Every feature is free, with no trial, subscription or paid tier. Install it on as many computers as you like.

Does it work offline?

Yes. You only need the internet to download it. Billing, printing and reports all work offline.

Where is my data stored?

On your computer, in Invoiceo’s data folder. Use Settings → Backup to keep a copy somewhere safe.

Which computers does it run on?

Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit), macOS 12 or later, and Linux (.deb or AppImage).

Can I use it outside India?

Yes. Choose your currency and language; GST fields can be turned off when you don’t need them.

How can it be free?

There’s no paid version. Optional paid customization and donations support development.

Is Invoiceo open source?

Yes. It is released under the MIT License, so anyone can read, use and change the code.

Do I need an account?

No online account. The app has its own login on your computer, so each person can have a password.

Does it support GST and UPI?

Yes — GSTIN, HSN/SAC, CGST + SGST or IGST, and your UPI QR code on invoices.

Can I move to a new computer?

Yes. Make a backup, install Invoiceo on the new computer and restore the backup there.

Is it right for a small shop?

Yes — barcode scanning, thermal receipts, stock tracking and daily sales reports are built in.

Why does Windows warn me when installing?

The installer isn’t code-signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.
